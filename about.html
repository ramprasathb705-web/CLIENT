Biography & Mission

Engineering Without Barriers

Advocating for digital equality by merging cutting-edge frontend engineering with empathetic, accessible design systems.

Hi, I’m Alex Morgan. Over the past 8+ years, I have architected mission-critical web applications for technology leaders, public sector organizations, and fintech platforms. My passion lies at the intersection of semantic HTML5 architecture, high-efficiency CSS, and assistive technology compatibility.

Too often, modern web applications sacrifice basic accessibility in favor of fragile JavaScript frameworks, unlabelled buttons, and unnavigable focus traps. I champion a different paradigm: accessibility-first engineering. By leveraging native semantic HTML elements before reaching for ARIA, applications remain naturally robust, fast, SEO-friendly, and accessible to screen readers, braille displays, and switch inputs.

I am an active contributor to the W3C Web Accessibility Initiative (WAI) community discussions and hold dual professional certifications from the IAAP.

FAQ

Accessibility Engineering Questions

Why do you prioritize semantic HTML over ARIA attributes?

Native HTML5 elements (such as <button>, <nav>, <dialog>) come pre-packaged with keyboard focusability, expected keyboard shortcuts (like Space and Enter activation), and inherent accessibility roles recognized by every modern assistive device. Using ARIA to recreate native functionality is prone to developer error and creates unnecessary maintenance overhead.

How do you ensure 100/100 Lighthouse Accessibility and SEO scores?

We enforce high-contrast color ratios (>4.5:1 for body copy and >3:1 for interactive graphics), provide meaningful and descriptive alt attributes on all meaningful images, ensure heading structures never skip levels (h1 followed by h2, then h3), include complete meta tags (title, description, canonical, robots), and embed Schema.org structured JSON-LD data.
